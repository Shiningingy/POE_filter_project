## The contradiction — you resolved it correctly, and for a better reason than you had

**Ruling: "a category's top tier" means the THEME CATEGORY — the unit that shares a style
row — never the tier-definition file.**

The reason is the rule's own logic. The exemption exists because *the top of a ladder is the
one thing in it a player is definitely walking to*. A ladder is precisely what a shared style
row describes. So the exemption can only attach to the ladder — and a file that is mid-ladder
inside its theme category has no top to claim.

Which means: **`Base Jewels` R4 stays cut.** You took the specific instruction over the
structural rule; under the corrected definition the two never disagreed — R4 is mid-ladder in
the `Jewels` theme category, so both the rule and the reason point the same way. Nothing to
restore.

**Please re-derive the 17 on that basis.** Two directions to check, not one:

- a file-level top tier that is mid-ladder in its theme category **loses** the exemption;
- a theme category whose *ladder* top sits at R3+ **keeps exactly one icon** — and if a
  ladder currently shows the exemption on two files, one of them is wrong.

Where several files share a row (flasks, heist, the league bases), the ladder is the row, and
the icon belongs to its top rung only. You were right that this grows more consequential as
the flat categories grow their ladders — which is the argument for settling it now rather than
per-case.
